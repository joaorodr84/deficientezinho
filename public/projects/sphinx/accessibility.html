Accessibility & how we try to make every table work

Sphinx is a party game — the whole point is that a room full of people with different abilities, ages, and reading levels can play it together. Here's where that's actually built in today, and where it still needs work.

No listening or speech recognition, ever

Correct, Pass, and every tick in Clear the List are the players' own honor-system judgment calls — the app never tries to listen to or parse what anyone says. That's a deliberate choice (speech recognition for a loud party room is unreliable at best), and a side effect is that nothing about scoring depends on being heard clearly or speaking in a particular way.

More than one way to answer

A Guess It or Banned Words round can be scored by swiping the card, tapping the Correct/Pass buttons, or — turned on in Settings — tilting the phone forward or back. No single motor movement is required to play.

Contrast-checked color themes

All six color themes (see Features) have their text and swipe colors checked against the WCAG AA 4.5:1 contrast threshold, not just picked for mood. A theme that looked good but read poorly against its own background got its colors adjusted, not shipped as-is.

Reading isn't required for every round

Image mode shows a picture instead of a word on categories that support it, and the Kids age band keeps the easiest, shortest words in every category — both aimed at players who can't yet read fluently.

Set your own pace

The round timer length is configurable, not fixed — a slower-paced group can give itself more time per round instead of being forced into the default clock.

What still needs work

  • Forehead mode and Tilt gestures both depend on holding or moving the phone in a specific way, which won't work for everyone — they're optional, never the only way to play.
  • No screen-reader pass has been done yet. This is a known gap, not a decision that it doesn't matter.

Tell us what's wrong

If something here doesn't hold up in practice, or you hit a wall this page doesn't mention, say so: joaorodr84@gmail.com. This is a one-person project — direct reports are how it actually gets fixed.
