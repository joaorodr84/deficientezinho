How it works

Every digit is a shape made of bars

DigiSpin's ten pieces are shaped like the digits 0 to 9, built from bars that sit on the lines between cells, the way a seven-segment display works. Place all ten on the board, rotating each one to fit, so that no two pieces ever overlap.

How to play

The in-app rules page, reused here so the two never drift apart

Every board below is drawn at load time by a small script that reimplements the app's own digit shapes, rotation, slot math and bar geometry — so a clash, a legal pairing, a dimmed given piece and a full solution all render the same way the real app draws them.

1The goal, the board and the pieces

Place all ten digit pieces, 0 to 9, on the board so that no two of their bars occupy the same spot. Every piece can be rotated to help it fit.

The pieces sit on the lines between the board's cells. Each digit is a shape made of short bars, the same shape as a seven-segment display: 0 is the four edges of one cell, 1 is a straight line with no width, and 2 to 9 each span two cells.

2Clashes

A clash is two pieces trying to use the same bar position on the board. DigiSpin won't allow it: the move is refused, the shared bar turns solid red, and a message names the other piece already using it.

digit 8 digit 4 digit 6 clash

The app's own first-time tutorial teaches this with a 7 and a 1: an upright 7 and a 1 dropped flat across it share two bars and get refused the same way.

3Controls

Drag a piece from the tray to place it on the board, or drag a piece already on the board to move it. Tap a piece in the tray to turn it a quarter turn before you place it.

4Clues

Some pieces may already be placed and locked for you, giving you a head start — the rest is up to you to work out.

The dimmed 5 is a given — already placed and locked. The full-colour 8 is a piece you placed yourself.

5Hint

Stuck? Hint places one remaining piece exactly where the solution has it, and locks it there. It costs one of a limited number of hints, shown next to the button.

Features

Everything the app does today

  • Five languagesEnglish, Português, Español, Français and Deutsch, or follow the device's own language automatically.
  • Light, dark and system themesFollows your device's appearance setting by default, or pick light or dark yourself in Settings — try it with the switch in the top right of this page.
  • Eight digit colour palettesThe original box-art colours, a colour-blind-friendly set, a high-contrast set for low vision, three gradient "mood" palettes (Ocean, Sunset, Forest), and two quiet monochrome ones (Dune, Dusk) where shape alone tells digits apart.
  • HintsStuck? Hint places one remaining piece exactly where the solution has it. A limited number per level, shown on the button.
  • Undo, Redo and RestartStep back or forward one move at a time, or send every piece you've placed back to the tray.
  • Replays and multiple solutionsSome levels have more than one valid layout. Replay a solved level to find another, and hold down any played level to see every solution you've found for it.
  • A daily puzzleOne puzzle a day, the same for every player, with a streak counter that tracks how many days in a row you've solved it.
  • Stats and achievementsLevels solved, solutions found, your fewest-move average, a daily streak, and achievements to earn — some of them secret.
  • Everything stays on your deviceNo accounts, no network calls, no analytics. Progress, stats and settings are saved locally until you delete the app.
Levels

250 boards, in ten packs of 25

Every board has at least one solution — finding all of them is up to you. Levels are grouped into ten colour-named packs of 25, one per digit, and every pack and every level can be played in any order, nothing is gated behind finishing earlier ones. Find every solution a level has and its square earns a star; master every level in a pack and the pack earns one too.

Accessibility

Accessibility & independent living

DigiSpin is built around independent living: the idea that a disability shouldn't decide whether you get to play, understand and enjoy something on your own terms, without having to lean on someone else's hands or eyes to get through it. That's a philosophy we hold across the apps we build, not a line that applies to just this one.

What that means in practice

Accessibility isn't something we add at the end — it's part of how DigiSpin is designed, alongside everything else. Today that means a choice of digit colour palettes built for colour blindness and for low vision, light, dark and system themes, and screen-reader support throughout the app.

We don't consider that finished. We're actively looking at a tap-based way to play that doesn't need a sustained drag, for anyone who finds that motion hard, and we're investigating what it would actually take for the board to be playable by touch and sound alone, without sight. Some of this exists today; some of it is still being worked out. Accessibility is a standing goal here, not a box we checked once and moved on from.

We want to hear from you

If something about DigiSpin gets in your way — a screen that's confusing, a contrast that's hard to read, a gesture that doesn't work for you — tell us. We'd rather hear it directly from someone it actually affects than guess at it ourselves.

The same goes for ideas. If there's a puzzle game you wish existed, or a way DigiSpin itself could be better, email info@deficientezinho.pt and tell us about it.
